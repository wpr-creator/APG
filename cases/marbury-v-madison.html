Marbury v. Madison (1803)

The Supreme Court established judicial review, the power to decide whether a law conflicts with the Constitution.

What happened?What was the constitutional issue?What did the Court decide?What are the decision’s limits?

What happened?

William Marbury was appointed a justice of the peace, but his appointment document was not delivered before President John Adams left office.

The new administration declined to deliver it. Marbury asked the Supreme Court to order Secretary of State James Madison to hand over the document, called a commission. The requested court order was a writ of mandamus: an order directing an official to perform a legal duty.

What was the constitutional issue?

Could Marbury bring this request directly to the Supreme Court under a law passed by Congress?

Article III limits the kinds of cases that can begin in the Supreme Court. That power to hear a case first is original jurisdiction. The Court read part of the Judiciary Act of 1789 as giving it authority to issue the requested order in an original proceeding. The question was what to do when that statute conflicted with the Constitution.

What did the Court decide?

The Court held that it could not issue the order because Congress could not expand its original jurisdiction beyond the Constitution.

Marshall concluded that Marbury had a right to the commission, but the Supreme Court lacked authority to grant this remedy in this proceeding. A law conflicting with the Constitution must give way. The Court therefore refused to apply the conflicting part of the Judiciary Act.

John Marshall · unanimous participating Court, 4–0

What are the decision’s limits?

Marbury established judicial review of acts of Congress; it did not give the Court unlimited power or give Marbury his commission.

Judicial review is a court’s power to decide whether a government action violates the Constitution. The important distinction is between having a legal right and bringing a claim to a court with authority to provide the requested remedy. The Court asserted the power to reject an unconstitutional statute while denying Marbury’s request.
