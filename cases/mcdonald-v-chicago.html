McDonald v. City of Chicago (2010)

The Second Amendment right to keep and bear arms applies to state and local governments.

What happened?What was the constitutional issue?What did the Court decide?What are the decision’s limits?

What happened?

Otis McDonald and other residents challenged Chicago’s restrictions that effectively prevented most residents from keeping handguns at home.

McDonald wanted a handgun for self-defense. An earlier Supreme Court decision had recognized an individual Second Amendment right to keep a handgun at home for that purpose, but that case involved Washington, D.C., rather than a state or city government.

What was the constitutional issue?

Does the Second Amendment’s protection for armed self-defense apply to state and local governments?

The Bill of Rights originally limited the federal government. Selective incorporation is the process of applying particular protections to states through the Fourteenth Amendment. McDonald asked the Court to apply the Second Amendment protection to Chicago.

What did the Court decide?

The Court held that the Second Amendment right recognized in the earlier case also applies to state and local governments.

Justice Alito’s opinion treated armed self-defense in the home as a fundamental right—a basic protection deeply rooted in the nation’s history and tradition. That was the reason for applying the right to states through the Fourteenth Amendment.

Four justices used the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, which protects against unfair government deprivation of life, liberty, or property. Justice Thomas supplied the fifth vote through the Amendment’s Privileges or Immunities Clause, a different protection for rights of U.S. citizenship. The Court reversed the lower court and returned the case for further proceedings.

Samuel Alito · judgment, 5–4

What are the decision’s limits?

McDonald applied this Second Amendment protection to the states; it did not complete incorporation of every Bill of Rights provision or invalidate every gun regulation.

For an AP comparison, identify who is being limited: the Second Amendment protection applies to federal, state, and local government. The decision did not resolve the constitutionality of every restriction on carrying, purchasing, or possessing a firearm.
