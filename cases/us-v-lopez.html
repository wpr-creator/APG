Unit 1

United States v. Lopez (1995)

Congress exceeded its power to regulate interstate commerce when it passed the federal ban at issue on guns in school zones.

What happened?What was the constitutional issue?What did the Court decide?What are the decision’s limits?

What happened?

Alfonso Lopez, a Texas high-school student, was charged under a federal law for bringing a gun to school.

The Gun-Free School Zones Act of 1990 made gun possession in a school zone a federal crime. Congress relied on its authority to regulate interstate commerce. Lopez challenged whether that authority reached the conduct covered by this law.

What was the constitutional issue?

Did Congress’s power over interstate commerce authorize this federal school-zone gun-possession law?

The Commerce Clause allows Congress to regulate commerce among the states. The government argued that guns near schools harmed education, which could harm the national economy. The question was whether that chain of effects supplied a sufficient connection to interstate commerce.

What did the Court decide?

The Court held that Congress exceeded its commerce power in this version of the law.

Possessing a gun near a school was not itself economic activity, and the law did not require a connection between the particular possession and interstate commerce. Accepting the government’s broad chain of reasoning would have left little distinction between national authority and the states’ general responsibility for public safety.

William Rehnquist · decision, 5–4

What are the decision’s limits?

Lopez limits the use of the Commerce Clause; it does not say Congress can never regulate guns or conduct that is not itself a sale.

Congress can regulate interstate trade routes, people and things moving in interstate commerce, and activities that substantially affect interstate commerce. The scope depends on the law and its constitutional basis. This case addressed the 1990 statute; Congress later revised the school-zone law to require a connection to interstate commerce.
