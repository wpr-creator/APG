Wisconsin v. Yoder (1972)

Wisconsin could not require the Amish families in this case to send their children to school beyond eighth grade.

What happened?What was the constitutional issue?What did the Court decide?What are the decision’s limits?

What happened?

Amish parents refused to send their children to formal school after eighth grade, although Wisconsin required attendance until age 16.

The families believed high-school attendance would conflict with their religious way of life. Their children continued practical learning within the community. Wisconsin convicted the parents, who argued that enforcing the attendance requirement against them burdened their religion.

What was the constitutional issue?

Could Wisconsin require these additional years of formal schooling despite the families’ religious objections?

The First Amendment’s Free Exercise Clause protects religious practice. The case required the Court to consider the burden on these families’ practice and the state’s interest in education—not simply whether parents preferred a different school arrangement.

What did the Court decide?

The Court held that Wisconsin could not enforce its attendance requirement against these Amish families in these circumstances.

The evidence showed a serious conflict with their religious practice and an alternative pattern of vocational learning. The Court concluded that Wisconsin had not shown a sufficiently strong need for two more years of formal schooling to overcome that burden. The ruling concerned a specific application of the law.

Warren Burger · judgment, 7–0; Douglas dissented in part

What are the decision’s limits?

Yoder did not create a general religious exemption from school or make the age of a religion the test for constitutional protection.

The result depended on the evidence and the limited additional schooling at issue. It does not mean every sincere religious objection defeats every generally applicable law. Keep the specific conflict, the families’ alternative education, and the Court’s narrow result together when explaining the case.
