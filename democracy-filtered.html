START WITH THE IDEA

REPUBLICANISM

People govern through elected representatives. Representation means those officials speak and act for people who cannot all make national policy directly.

PEOPLE'S VOICES→REPRESENTATIVES→PUBLIC POLICY

ESSENTIAL QUESTION: How does representative democracy decide whose voices matter?

THE SCENARIO Congress is considering new rules and regulations for social-media platforms. Millions of people have opinions, organizations are pushing competing plans, and elected officials must decide what to do.

THREE DEMOCRATIC FILTERS

0 OF 3 FILTERS TESTED

WHY THESE THREE? In the social-media scenario, Congress cannot listen equally to millions of individual voices, every organization, and every expert. These three models show three different ways elected representatives could decide whose voices carry the most influence.

  1. Participatory: Listen broadly to individual citizens.
  2. Pluralist: Weigh organizations competing over the policy.
  3. Elite / filtered: Trust elected leaders and experts to decide.

YOUR JOB: Test each route from public voice to government policy. Notice who becomes louder, who may be missed, and which model you think Congress should use.

PARTICIPATORY DEMOCRACY

“KEEP GOVERNMENT CLOSE.”

People should have many direct ways to influence representatives. A republic that becomes too large and distant stops hearing ordinary citizens.

PEOPLE→DIRECT ACTION→GOVERNMENT
LOOK FOR IT TODAYTown hallsProtestsPrimariesBallot initiatives

AMPLIFIES Broad citizen participation

CAN MISS People with less time, access, or political confidence

PLURALIST DEMOCRACY

“LET INTERESTS COMPETE.”

Factions are unavoidable. In a large republic, many organized interests compete, making it harder for one faction to dominate everyone else.

PEOPLE→GROUPS COMPETE→GOVERNMENT
LOOK FOR IT TODAYInterest groupsUnionsCoalitionsLobbying

AMPLIFIES Organized interests and coalitions

CAN MISS Interests without equal money, membership, or access

Which situation best fits Madison’s filter?

ELITE / FILTERED DEMOCRACY

“LET INSTITUTIONS REFINE.”

Representatives and durable institutions should deliberate, use expertise, and turn public demands into workable policy instead of simply echoing every immediate impulse.

PEOPLE→LEADERS + EXPERTS→POLICY
LOOK FOR IT TODAYCommitteesAgenciesCourtsTrustee representation

AMPLIFIES Expertise, deliberation, and institutional stability

CAN MISS Ordinary people far from powerful decision-makers
