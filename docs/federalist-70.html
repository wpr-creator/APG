Energy, unity, and accountability: Hamilton’s argument for one president who can act—and answer for the result.

Author and setting

Congress makes federal laws. The president leads the executive branch, which carries those laws out through government departments and agencies. Hamilton’s question was whether one person or several people should share leadership of that branch.

Alexander Hamilton wrote Federalist No. 70 as Publius during the debate over approving the Constitution. Critics feared that a powerful president could threaten liberty.

Hamilton argued that one president could lead effectively and be held responsible. His central question was why one person, rather than several executives, should lead the executive branch.

The meaning and purposes of energy

Energy means the ability to make decisions and carry them out effectively. Hamilton argued that an energetic executive was necessary for good government.

“Energy in the Executive is a leading character in the definition of good government.”Alexander Hamilton · Federalist No. 70

Why energy matters

  • National defense — Respond when the country faces attack.
  • Enforcing laws — Make sure federal laws are carried out.
  • Protecting property — Protect people’s possessions and property rights.
  • Securing liberty — Protect people’s freedom and rights.

Four ingredients of executive energy

Hamilton identified four things the president’s office needed to work effectively. He called these the ingredients of executive energy.

Hamilton’s terms, in plain language
Hamilton’s termStudent explanation
UnityOne president leads the executive branch. Advisers help, but do not share equal executive authority.
DurationA president needs time in office to carry out decisions and follow through on plans.
Adequate supportReliable financial support lets the president perform the job and reduces others’ ability to apply financial pressure.
Competent powersThe president needs enough legal authority to carry out the responsibilities of the office.

Competent means suited to the job. Hamilton’s original wording for adequate support is an adequate provision for its support.

Four advantages of unity

Unity is the first ingredient. Hamilton then explained four advantages he believed came from placing executive leadership in one person.

Advantages Hamilton associates with one executive
TermMeaning
DecisionChoosing a course of action.
ActivityPutting that choice into action.
SecrecyKeeping sensitive plans confidential when necessary.
DispatchActing promptly when delay could cause harm.

The original text spells dispatch as despatch.

Accountability and two safeguards

Accountability means being answerable for official actions. Hamilton argued that one executive makes responsibility easier to identify.

Several executives

In our hypothetical council, the two awake presidents wanted to respond but needed another president’s approval. After the failure, each leader could blame the others or the voting rule.

One president

With one president directing the response, the public can identify whose decisions to question. Several executives could blame one another.

Two safeguards for republican government

  • Dependence on the people — Elections give the public a recurring voice in who leads. A president who loses public support may lose the opportunity for another term.
  • Responsibility for official conduct — People can examine and criticize presidential decisions. Serious misconduct can lead to investigation, impeachment by the House, and a Senate trial.

Lesson EQ connection

How can a president lead decisively without opening the door to tyranny?

In Federalist No. 70, Hamilton argued that one president could lead decisively through clear direction and sufficient powers. Dependence on the people and responsibility for official conduct help guard against opening the door to tyranny.
