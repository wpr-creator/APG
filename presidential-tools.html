APG LESSON 2.3 / AP TOPICS 2.4–2.7

The Presidential Toolbox

Presidents use formal and informal powers to lead the executive branch and carry out their policy goals.

Formal powers

Powers the Constitution gives the president, such as rejecting bills and commanding the armed forces.

Formal powers
ToolWhat the president can do
Regular veto

Reject a bill and return it to Congress with reasons.

Check or limitCongress can override the veto with a two-thirds vote in each chamber.

Pocket veto

Leave a bill unsigned for 10 days, excluding Sundays, while Congress’s absence prevents its return. The bill fails.

Check or limitCongress cannot override it; lawmakers must pass the bill again. If the bill can be returned, inaction makes it law after 10 days.

Treaty

Negotiate a formal agreement with another country.

Check or limitTwo-thirds of senators present must consent before the president formally approves it.

Informal powers

Ways presidents influence government through political practice, such as negotiating with lawmakers or asking the public to support a proposal.

Informal powers
ToolWhat the president can do
Executive agreement

Make an agreement with another country without the Senate’s two-thirds treaty vote.

Check or limitMust rely on authority from the Constitution, a law, or an earlier treaty. Congress controls needed funding.

Executive order

Give executive officials written directions for carrying out government work.

Check or limitMust follow existing law. Courts can block orders that exceed presidential authority.

Signing statement

Explain how the administration interprets or plans to carry out a law when signing it.

Check or limitDoes not change the law or remove its requirements.

Bargaining and persuasion

Negotiate with lawmakers and seek their support for proposals.

Check or limitLawmakers choose how to vote; the president cannot command their support.

Bully pulpit

Use public attention to highlight issues and build support for proposals.

Check or limitPublic support does not guarantee action; Congress still decides whether to pass legislation.
