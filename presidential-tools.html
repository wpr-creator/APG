APG LESSON 2.3 / AP TOPICS 2.4–2.7

The Presidential Toolbox

Formal powers

Powers the Constitution gives the president, such as rejecting bills and commanding the armed forces.

Informal powers

Ways presidents influence government through political practice, such as negotiating with lawmakers or asking the public to support a proposal.

Presidential tools
ToolWhat the president can doMain check or limit
Regular vetoReject a bill and return it to Congress with reasons.Congress can override the veto with a two-thirds vote in each chamber.
Pocket vetoLeave a bill unsigned for 10 days, excluding Sundays, while Congress’s absence prevents its return. The bill fails.Congress cannot override it; lawmakers must pass the bill again. If the bill can be returned, inaction makes it law after 10 days.
TreatyNegotiate a formal agreement with another country.Two-thirds of senators present must consent before the president formally approves it.
Executive agreementMake an agreement with another country without the Senate’s two-thirds treaty vote.Must rely on authority from the Constitution, a law, or an earlier treaty. Congress controls needed funding.
Executive orderGive executive officials written directions for carrying out government work.Must follow existing law. Courts can block orders that exceed presidential authority.
Signing statementExplain how the administration interprets or plans to carry out a law when signing it.Does not change the law or remove its requirements.
Bargaining and persuasionNegotiate with lawmakers and seek their support for proposals.Lawmakers choose how to vote; the president cannot command their support.
Bully pulpitUse public attention to highlight issues and build support for proposals.Public support does not guarantee action; Congress still decides whether to pass legislation.
