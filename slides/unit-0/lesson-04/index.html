Kanye West once said, “No one man should have all that power.”

A country ruled by one person might sound dangerous. But what if that person were a good leader who cared about the people? Decisions could happen quickly. No gridlock. No endless arguments. No waiting for everyone to agree.

Maybe dividing power creates problems too.

SET THE STAGE

The United States chose rule by many. The CONSTITUTION divides the power of the national government among three branches, each with a different role to play.

 

ARTICLE I gives us CONGRESS. ARTICLE II gives executive power to the PRESIDENT. ARTICLE III creates the federal courts, including the SUPREME COURT.

 

Together, they form the LEGISLATIVE, EXECUTIVE, and JUDICIAL branches. Dividing government power among them is a core principle called SEPARATION OF POWERS.

 

No single branch gets the whole stage.

##

MEET THE CAST

So who actually fills the three branches?

 

CONGRESS has two houses. The HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES has 435 members, while the SENATE has 100, two from each state.

 

The EXECUTIVE BRANCH is led by the PRESIDENT, VICE PRESIDENT, and CABINET, but includes more than 4 million civilian and military workers across the federal government.

 

At the top of the JUDICIAL BRANCH sits the SUPREME COURT, made up of nine justices.

 

Three branches. A very large cast.

##

KNOW THE SCRIPT

Dividing power is only the beginning. The branches can also limit one another through CHECKS AND BALANCES.

 

  • CONGRESS can override presidential vetoes, control spending, impeach officials, and approve key presidential appointments.
  • The PRESIDENT can veto legislation and nominate federal judges, including SUPREME COURT justices.
  • The COURTS use JUDICIAL REVIEW to determine whether government actions follow the CONSTITUTION.

 

To paraphrase Ye, “No one branch should have all that power."

STAGE DIRECTIONS

Not everyone thought the original CONSTITUTION offered enough protection. Some FOUNDING FATHERS demanded stronger guarantees before they were willing to support the new government. The result was the BILL OF RIGHTS.

 

  • 1ST speech, religion, press, assembly, petition
  • 2ND right to bear arms
  • 4TH protection from unreasonable searches
  • 5TH due process and protections for the accused
  • 6TH rights during a criminal trial
  • 8TH limits on punishments and bail

 

Even the government has to follow the rules.

##

SCRIPT REWRITE

Even the BILL OF RIGHTS did not settle everything. Over the next 200 years, Americans AMENDED the CONSTITUTION 17 more times, changing the rules as the country changed.

 

The 13TH AMENDMENT abolished slavery. Several amendments that followed addressed citizenship, legal protections, and access to voting.

 

  • 14TH citizenship, due process, equal protection
  • 15TH voting rights regardless of race
  • 19TH voting rights regardless of sex
  • 24TH no poll tax in federal elections
  • 26TH voting age lowered to 18

##

STAGE NOTES

The CONSTITUTION divides and limits government power so no one person or branch controls the whole system.

  • SEPARATION OF POWERS → three branches with different roles
  • CHECKS AND BALANCES → each branch can limit the others
  • BILL OF RIGHTS and later AMENDMENTS → protect and expand individual rights
