Before we go further, what does REPUBLICANISM mean?

REPUBLICANISM means that we elect representatives to make decisions for us instead of voting on every issue ourselves.

A Replubic, if...

Last lesson, we studied six democratic ideals. A DEMOCRATIC IDEAL is a belief about how democracy should work.

One ideal was REPUBLICANISM. It means that people govern through elected representatives.

A REPRESENTATIVE DEMOCRACY is the system that puts this ideal into practice. Citizens elect officials to make public decisions for them.

But representatives cannot follow every request. How can the system live up to the promise that the people should govern?

Three models offer different answers.

MODEL 01

We The People

One model of representative democracy is PARTICIPATORY DEMOCRACY. A model is a way of explaining how a political system works.

This model says government works best when ordinary people take an active role.

Citizens participate by voting, contacting officials, attending meetings, protesting, joining campaigns, and sometimes voting directly on policies.

Representatives should remain close to the people and respond to what citizens say they want.

More participation gives the public a stronger voice.

Strength in Numbers

A second model of representative democracy is PLURALIST DEMOCRACY. It says people gain influence by joining others who share their concerns.

A FACTION forms around a shared interest. Modern factions often work through INTEREST GROUPS. The AARP represents older Americans, the Sierra Club supports environmental protection, and the NRA supports gun-ownership rights.

These groups collect money, contact officials, run advertisements, and support candidates.

Organization can make a shared voice harder to ignore.

“Liberty is to faction what air is to fire.”

James Madison, Federalist No. 10 (1787)

Fight fire with fire

In FEDERALIST NO. 10, Madison argued that LIBERTY—people’s freedom to hold different beliefs and organize around shared interests—allows FACTIONS to form, just as air feeds fire. Taking away that freedom would be worse than the factions themselves.

His solution was a large REPUBLIC with so many competing factions that no single group could easily take control. As Madison wrote, “Ambition must be made to counteract ambition.”

Madison’s argument supports A PLURALIST MODEL of representative democracy.

MODEL 03

A Small Number

PARTICIPATORY DEMOCRACY brings individuals into the process. PLURALIST DEMOCRACY brings organized groups. A third model, ELITE DEMOCRACY, narrows the funnel: many people may express opinions, but a much smaller group does the detailed work of governing.

Elected leaders rely on judges, administrators, scientists, economists, and other experts who understand complicated issues. Supporters believe this produces more informed and steady decisions.

But is limiting decision-making to a few voices ANTITHETICAL to the ideal of REPUBLICANISM?

An Intermediate Body

The Constitution includes several features that place decisions in the hands of a smaller group:

  • The ELECTORAL COLLEGE formally selects the president.
  • The SENATE gives two members from each state longer six-year terms.
  • FEDERAL JUDGES are appointed rather than elected and serve for life.
  • The president appoints officials and experts to lead EXECUTIVE AGENCIES.

These features give decision-makers space to use their own judgment, but the ELITE MODEL rarely operates alone; elections, citizens, and organized groups still influence them.

LESSON RECAP

American representative democracy blends three routes of influence: citizens participate directly, organized groups compete, and leaders and experts make the final decisions.

  • REPUBLICANISM gives citizens a voice through elected representatives.
  • PARTICIPATORY DEMOCRACY offers direct access, but individual voices may get lost in the crowd.
  • PLURALIST DEMOCRACY amplifies organized groups, but those groups do not have equal resources.
  • ELITE DEMOCRACY relies on experienced leaders and experts, but places final decisions in fewer hands.
